# Executive summary

ForestFormer3D, a published deep-learning model for individual tree segmentation in forest
LiDAR, was applied without retraining to Berlin's open 2021 airborne laser scanning (ALS)
over the Tegel and Spandau forests: {{methods.ff3d.tiles}} km tiles, about 580 million
points (4-27 million per tile), 20-30 points/m² where it is forest. The result is one seamless mosaic of
**{{methods.ff3d.trees}} tree instances**, each with a position, a top height, a crown
polygon, a point count and a confidence score, delivered as LAS point clouds, GeoPackage
tables, GeoTIFF rasters, a QGIS project and a web viewer. Two other methods were run on
exactly the same inputs through exactly the same tiling and merging: SegmentAnyTree, the
other published deep model for this task, and AMS3D, a classical adaptive mean-shift
baseline.

What the work established:

* **Scale works.** Splitting each km tile into 100 m sub-tiles with a 20 m halo and merging
  all sub-tiles of the whole mosaic in one pass gives tree ids that are unique and dense
  across sub-tile and km-tile borders alike; the unlabelled strip along the grid lines is
  gone ({{stitch.ff3d.strip_mean|3}} percentage points excess, from 6.3 before), and
  {{stitch.ff3d.n_unified}} instances were unified over the halos. Inference took about
  1.5 GPU-hours per km tile on an H100 (SegmentAnyTree 1.9, AMS3D 22 CPU-minutes on 48 cores).
* **The two deep models largely agree, and disagree in a characteristic way.** On
  {{agreement.ff3d_sat.n_points}} identical points, {{agreement.ff3d_sat.matched}} tree pairs
  overlap with IoU ≥ 0.5 ({{agreement.ff3d_sat.matched_frac_a|pct}} of ForestFormer3D's trees,
  {{agreement.ff3d_sat.matched_frac_b|pct}} of SegmentAnyTree's; median IoU
  {{agreement.ff3d_sat.iou_median|3}}). Where they differ, SegmentAnyTree has usually cut one
  crown into several ({{agreement.ff3d_sat.split_a_frac|pct}} of ForestFormer3D trees are
  covered by two or more SegmentAnyTree instances, {{agreement.ff3d_sat.split_b_frac|pct}} the
  other way round). SegmentAnyTree reports {{sat_over_ff3d|3}}x as many trees overall; the
  surplus is concentrated in the Spandau forest (ratio {{ratio_by_footprint.R13.ratio|2}}
  there against {{ratio_by_footprint.R12.ratio|2}} in Tegel). A fourth segmentation,
  Stefan Reder's PointTreeFormer on {{agreement.ff3d_ptf.tiles}} Tegel tiles, matches
  {{agreement.ff3d_ptf.matched_frac_a|pct}} of ForestFormer3D's trees and draws the larger
  crowns ({{agreement.ff3d_ptf.split_b_frac|pct}} of its trees are covered by several
  ForestFormer3D instances, {{agreement.ff3d_ptf.split_a_frac|pct}} the other way).
* **The predictions are consistent with the forest inventory.** Joined to the Berlin forest
  stand map, the 90th percentile of predicted tree heights per stand tracks the 2014
  inventory's canopy height by species (correlation {{stands_r|2}} over {{stands_n}}
  stands, {{stands_bias_p90|1}} m above it on average -- seven years of growth lie between
  the two), with densities of 280-400 trees/ha for the main species under a pine-dominated canopy.
* **The street and park trees are found.** Of {{cadastre.ff3d.n_cadastre}} trees of the
  Berlin tree cadastre inside the mosaic, {{cadastre.ff3d.detected_frac|pct0}} have a
  ForestFormer3D tree top within 3 m ({{cadastre.sat.detected_frac|pct0}} for
  SegmentAnyTree), with no height bias ({{cadastre.ff3d.height_bias|2}} m) and a mean
  absolute height difference of {{cadastre.ff3d.height_mae|1}} m against the cadastre's
  inspection estimates.
* **Two artefacts need a post-filter.** The Berlin ALS has no building class, so the model
  segments roof vegetation and roofs as trees: masking the official ALKIS building
  footprints removes {{buildings.removed_frac|pct}} of the instances (up to 40 % on the
  densest tile). And {{methods.ff3d.flat}} instances are flat, wide blobs -- bare ground
  labelled as vegetation -- that a height threshold would remove.

What it did not establish: which method is *right* inside the forest. No per-tree ground
truth exists there; all method-to-method figures are agreement, not accuracy. The WINMOL
field circles (974 hand-delineated crowns in Revier 12 and 13) are the one species-labelled
reference on these tiles and are the obvious next step. Both survey footprints, R12
Tegelsee and R13 Spandau, are now entirely inside the mosaic.
