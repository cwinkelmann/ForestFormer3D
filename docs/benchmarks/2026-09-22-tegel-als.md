## 3. Reading the numbers

- Tree count vs CHM baseline: the CHM local-maxima count (1 m cells, 3 m window, 3 m
  minimum height, from the ALS classes only) is a crude reference that over-counts broad
  crowns and under-counts suppressed trees; agreement within +-50 % is what we ask of a
  first pass. Both tiles pass comfortably: 402 vs 397 on r12 (+1.3 %) and 443 vs 400 on
  r13 (+10.8 %). The model finds about as many stems as the crown maxima suggest, so on
  ALS density it is neither collapsing everything into a few giant instances nor
  shattering crowns wholesale.
- Height distribution: model heights are `top_z` minus the 1 m ground grid at the stem;
  compare the median with the CHM maxima median. Both tiles come out 3-4 m low (25.8 vs
  29.1 m on r12, 24.6 vs 28.6 m on r13) while the *maxima* agree exactly (34.8 m and
  35.5 m). The model minimum of 2.2 m against a CHM minimum of 12.5 / 14.0 m is the real
  difference: the model emits a tail of very short instances that the 3 m-minimum CHM
  filter never produces, and those drag the median down. That tail is the classic
  signature of crown fragments being scored as separate trees, and it is consistent with
  a TLS/ULS-trained model reading a ~20-29 pts/m2 canopy it has never seen.
- Ground vs vegetation: the 2x2 table compares model `semantic == 0` with ALS class 2.
  Agreement is 97.7 % (r12) and 98.2 % (r13) with no nodata points at all, so the
  semantic head transfers to ALS essentially intact. What remains is asymmetric - the
  model over-calls ground (3,075 and 4,767 vegetation points labelled ground) far more
  often than it misses it (1,277 and 354) - which is what low-density understory looks
  like when the model expects TLS stem returns. The failure is in the instance head, not
  the semantic head.
- Observations to confirm visually (the QGIS screenshots in section 2 are still to be
  captured by hand; **this visual check is pending** and the statements here are inferred
  from the numbers alone): expect fragmented crowns splitting into a low-height instance
  plus a full-height one - that is where the 2.2 m minimum and the depressed median come
  from; expect the ground over-call to show as ground-coloured points reaching up into
  low vegetation; the exact match of the maximum heights on both tiles means the tallest
  crowns are being captured whole, so merged neighbours and cylinder-border cuts are
  expected to be the minor effects rather than the dominant ones.

## 4. Recommendation on ALS fine-tuning

Decision rule (implemented in `ff3d_geo.report.recommend`): the checkpoint is usable as a
first pass on ALS if, per tile, the model tree count is within 50 % of the CHM baseline
count and the median model height is within 3 m of the median CHM maxima height. If either
tile fails either criterion, fine-tuning on ALS is recommended.

| Tile | Trees (model / CHM) | Median height (model / CHM, m) | First pass usable |
|---|---|---|---|
| r12 Tegel | 402 / 397 | 25.8 / 29.1 | no |
| r13 Spandau | 443 / 400 | 24.6 / 28.6 | no |

Recommendation: fine-tune on ALS. The tree-count criterion passes on both tiles (+1.3 %
on r12, +10.8 % on r13, both far inside the 50 % band), but the median-height criterion
fails on both: r12 is 3.3 m below the CHM median and r13 is 4.0 m below, each outside the
3 m tolerance. Since the maximum heights match the CHM exactly on both tiles, the failure
is not a systematic vertical offset but a tail of short spurious instances, i.e. an
instance-head problem; the semantic head is fine at 97.7 / 98.2 % ground agreement and
needs no attention. Fine-tuning itself is out of scope for this phase; if recommended,
the next step is a small ALS-labelled set (e.g. tiles with manually checked GeoPackage
rows) and a `radius`/`voxel_size` sweep for the ~20-29 pts/m2 density, plus a look at
`model.test_cfg.score_th` since raising it is the cheapest way to cut the short-instance
tail without retraining.
